A fictional course needs no map. But if you are drawing a real property, or routing a course over real land, **Map → Place course on map…** pins the drawing to the Earth.

Search for a place in the United States, or paste coordinates or a map link. The middle of your course goes on the spot you choose. Your drawing does not move; a satellite layer appears underneath it.

## Why it lines up

The course is already in yards. Placing it only records the latitude and longitude of its top-left corner. Map tiles use the Web Mercator projection, which stretches distances the further you are from the equator, so the app corrects for that at the course's latitude. Across a golf property the error is far below a yard.

## Imagery, and what you may do with it

| Source | Detail | License |
|---|---|---|
| USGS aerial (default) | About 2 m per pixel | United States public domain. Free to trace and to use commercially. |
| Esri World Imagery | Sharper | Free for personal, non-commercial use only. |

Google's maps are deliberately not offered; their terms forbid tracing.

The map, like an underlay image, is **reference material**. It is never included in exports.

## Adjusting

Select the map layer and use the **Move** tool (U) to slide the map under the course, or the arrow keys for one-yard nudges. The Info panel has the opacity and the imagery source.
