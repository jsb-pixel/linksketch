## Autosave is a convenience, not a backup

Linksketch keeps your current course **in your browser, on your computer**, and brings it back when you return. Nothing is uploaded.

That copy can vanish if you clear your browsing data, use a private window, or move to another browser or computer. The status bar says so until you save.

## Saving a file

**File → Save** (⌘S) writes a `.fairway` file. It is an ordinary file: back it up, move it, email it. **File → Open** (⌘O) reads one back, and so does dragging one onto the window.

A `.fairway` file holds everything: paint, shapes, holes, tee sets, labels, colors, terrain, players and where the course sits on the map. Files from older versions always open in newer ones.

## Installing it

In Chrome or Edge, use the install button in the address bar. In Safari, use **File → Add to Dock**. Linksketch then opens in its own window like any other app.

## Working offline

Once you have opened the editor, it **works without a connection**. Drawing, holes, strategy, stats and exports all run on your computer.

Three things need the internet, because they fetch data from others: **satellite imagery**, **elevation from USGS**, and **place search**. Those are never stored for offline use.

## Privacy

There is no account, no tracking and no analytics. The only requests the editor makes are for map tiles, elevation and place search, and only when you use those features.
