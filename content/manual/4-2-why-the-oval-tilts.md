The pattern is not square to the line of play. It leans.

{{fig tilt | TILT | A right-hander's pattern runs from short-right to long-left. A left-hander's is the mirror image.}}

## Where the lean comes from

For a right-handed player, a miss to the right is usually a shot that curved right: a fade or a slice. That curve comes with extra backspin and a glancing strike, so the ball **loses distance**. A miss to the left usually curved left: a draw or a hook. Those fly lower and **run further**.

Put together, the misses line up along a diagonal: short on the right, long on the left.

## Why it matters when you design

It changes which hazards are really in play.

- A bunker **short and right** of a green sits right where a right-hander's weak miss goes. It catches far more shots than its size suggests.
- The same bunker **long and right** is almost never found.
- A pond **long and left** punishes the pulled shot that runs.

The default tilt is 15 degrees. Tick **Lefty** for a player and the pattern mirrors. Both are under **Strategy → Players**.
